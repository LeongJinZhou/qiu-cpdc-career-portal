## 1. What this platform is

QIU Career Hub is the university's record of everything you do for your career while you study, and the place where that record turns into something useful.

It holds:

- the workshops and sessions you attend,
- the hours you work on placement or internship, approved by your workplace supervisor,
- the online courses you complete and prove,
- your Final Year Project (the major piece of work in your last year),
- and a CV built automatically from all of the above.

It also connects the university to employers: verified employer partners post vacancies here and can browse student projects that students chose to publish.

**Two things are worth understanding before anything else.**

**Only verified activity counts.** Booking a workshop is not attending it; you also have to check in on the day. Logging placement hours is not the same as having them approved; your workplace supervisor has to approve them. Downloading a course certificate is not the same as having it verified; a person at CPDC has to check it. This is deliberate — it is exactly what makes the record worth showing to an employer.

**You only see what your role allows.** There are eleven kinds of account, and each one has a different menu. If a colleague mentions a screen you do not have, that is the design working, not a fault.

## 2. Signing in and finding your way

### Signing in

1. Open the sign-in page.
2. Press **Continue with Google**.
3. Choose the Google account whose email address the university (or CPDC) registered for you.
4. Read the permission prompt and continue. QIU Career Hub uses Google SSO; it does not create a separate QIU password.
5. If Google says the account is not allowed, contact [CPDC contact — to be filled in by CPDC]. Do not create a second account — your record is attached to the first one.

After signing in you land on the screen that matches your role. Students land on the Dashboard, most staff land on Admin, and employers land on Employers.

### Finding a screen

1. Look at the menu down the left of the screen. On a phone, look at the bar across the bottom and tap the menu button for the full list.
2. Read the labels — they are named after the thing you want, for example **Logbook** for placement hours or **My CV** for your CV.
3. If a screen is not in your menu, your role does not have access to it.
4. Open **Help** and search for the task instead. Each guide says which roles it is for.

If you open a web address your role cannot use, the platform sends you back to your own home screen rather than showing an error.

### The welcome walkthrough

The first time you sign in you get a short walkthrough for your role. You can see it again at any time: open **Help** and press **Show me around again**. Replaying it changes nothing about your record.

## 3. Start here, by role

There are eleven account roles, plus one person who works with the platform without an account at all: the workplace supervisor who approves placement hours.

### 3.1 Student

**Who you are:** you are studying at QIU. This platform records what you do for your career alongside your degree, and turns it into a CV you can send to employers.

**Your first five minutes**

1. Open **Settings** and check your faculty and programme are right. Everything else is filtered by these two.
2. Add one or two career targets — the kind of job you want after you graduate. Plain words are fine.
3. Open **Diagnostics** and take the short skills test for your faculty.
4. Open **Workshops** and register for the first session your results suggest.
5. Open **My CV** to see the draft already being built for you.

**The screens that matter most**

| Screen | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| Dashboard | Shows the single next thing to do. |
| Career Path | What to do in each year of your degree. |
| Logbook | Where you add placement hours for supervisor approval. |
| My CV | Your CV, built from verified activity, exportable as a PDF. |
| Employers | Jobs and internships from employers hiring QIU students. |

**What you can see:** your own record only. Staff at your faculty can see your progress because they support and assess you. Employers see only what you choose to publish and a coordinator approves.

### Assessments and tests

Students open **Assessments & Tests** to take a published test. A test appears only when it matches the student's faculty or programme. Answer every question, press **Submit assessment**, and the score and pass status are saved to the student record. Students never receive the answer key.

Lecturers and CPDC officers open the same area to upload a PDF, Word document or UTF-8 text file. Number each question, put options on separate `A.` / `B.` / `C.` / `D.` lines, and include an explicit `Answer: B` line. The upload becomes a draft. Read every question and answer key before publishing. Image-only scanned PDFs need OCR first because the platform cannot read a photograph without selectable text. The original file is not stored; the source name, type, hash, parsed questions and audit history are stored.

### 3.2 Alumni Mentor

**Who you are:** you graduated from QIU. You are here to advise current students, keep learning, and stay connected.

**Your first five minutes**

1. Open **Settings** and add your current industry and job role, so students can tell whether you are the right person to ask.
2. Open **Network** and publish the times you are willing to be booked for.
3. Read the connection requests waiting for you.
4. Open **Talent Feed** to see what current students are building.

**Key screens:** Network (mentoring requests and availability), Talent Feed (published student projects), Workshops (attend or offer to host), Learning (courses you can take and have verified).

**What you can see:** published student work and the students who contact you. Not academic records, grades or placement hours.

### 3.3 Head of Programme

**Who you are:** you lead a programme and are accountable for whether its students are ready for work when they graduate.

**Your first five minutes**

1. Open **Admin** and read the list of students needing employability support in your programme.
2. Open **Logbook** and check how many placement entries are still waiting on a workplace supervisor.
3. Open **FYP** and clear any final-year projects waiting for approval.
4. Open **Workshops** to see what is scheduled for your students this term.

**Key screens:** Admin (programme-level view of who is falling behind), Career Path (the path your students follow), Logbook (placement hours across the programme), Talent Feed (what your programme is showing employers).

**What you can see:** students on your programme, and no other programme. Every record you open is logged.

### 3.4 Faculty Coordinator

**Who you are:** the academic lead for a faculty. You approve student work and keep your faculty's students moving.

**Your first five minutes**

1. Open **Admin** and check the queue of items waiting for your decision.
2. Open **FYP** and review the final-year projects submitted by your faculty.
3. Open **Learning** and clear the certificate verification queue.
4. Open **Logbook** and chase the placement entries that have been pending longest.

**Key screens:** Admin (your approval queue), Logbook (faculty placement hours), Workshops (faculty sessions, and publishing new ones), Talent Feed (what you have approved for employers).

**What you can see:** the faculty you are set to. Approving a final-year project is what makes it visible outside QIU, so check it before you approve.

### 3.5 Academic Supervisor

**Who you are:** you supervise a specific group of students through their internship or fieldwork. Your view is deliberately small.

**Your first five minutes**

1. Open **Admin** and read the list of students assigned to you.
2. Open **Logbook** and look at each student's placement entries.
3. Note which entries have waited longest on a workplace supervisor, and tell those students to follow up.
4. Check each student's total approved hours against what their programme requires.

**Key screens:** Admin (your assigned students), Logbook (their hours and approval status), Workshops (sessions to recommend).

**What you can see:** only your assigned students. Approval of placement hours comes from the workplace supervisor who saw the work, not from you.

### 3.6 Placement Officer

**Who you are:** you arrange industrial training placements and make sure worked hours are properly recorded and approved.

**Your first five minutes**

1. Open **Logbook** and find every entry still waiting for a workplace supervisor.
2. Check the ones that have waited longest, and find out whether the supervisor's email address was wrong.
3. Open **Employers** to see which organisations are currently offering placements.
4. Open **Admin** to see which students have no placement activity at all yet.

**Key screens:** Logbook (every placement entry and its status), Employers (partner organisations and roles), Admin (students with no placement progress), Workshops (pre-placement briefings).

**What you can see:** placement activity for your faculty. A workplace supervisor never gets an account — they approve through a single expiring link, so a wrong email address is the usual reason an approval never arrives.

### 3.7 CPDC Officer

**Who you are:** career services staff. You run the workshops, verify the evidence, approve employers, and keep the whole thing moving.

**Your first five minutes**

1. Open **Admin** and read the operational queue of what needs attention today.
2. Open **Learning** and clear the certificate verification queue.
3. Open **Workshops** and check this week's sessions have a date, a venue and enough seats.
4. Open **Employers** and approve or reject any organisation waiting for verification.

**Key screens:** Admin (university-wide queue and analytics), Workshops (publishing and check-in), Learning (certificate verification), Employers (verification and vacancies), Logbook (placement approvals).

**What you can see:** students across the university. That access is logged, and it exists so you can support students — not to browse.

### 3.8 Career Counselor

**Who you are:** you do the one-to-one work — CV reviews, mock interviews and helping students work out what they are aiming at.

**Your first five minutes**

1. Open **Admin** to see which students have been flagged as needing support.
2. Open **My CV** to look at a student's CV before a review session, so you arrive prepared.
3. Open **Diagnostics** to see what a student's skills test actually said.
4. Open **Workshops** and check what you can refer a student to this month.

**Key screens:** Admin (flagged students), CV review, Interview Practice (timed practice you can set students), Diagnostics.

**What you can see:** student career records across the university, and every record you open is logged. Coaching notes belong in your own case records, not in the student's public profile.

### 3.9 Registrar

**Who you are:** you handle academic clearance and transcripts. You use this platform as evidence of what a student completed, not as a place to change it.

**Your first five minutes**

1. Open **Admin** and find the student you are clearing.
2. Open **Logbook** and check their approved placement hours. Pending hours do not count.
3. Open **My CV** for that student to see the verified achievements on record.
4. Record your decision in the university's own clearance process.

**Key screens:** Admin (finding a student), Logbook (approved hours), CV (the verified achievement record).

**What you can see:** read-only academic evidence across the university. Every record you open is written to the audit log.

### 3.10 Super-Admin

**Who you are:** you govern the platform itself — who has which role, which areas are switched on, and whether the audit trail is intact.

**Your first five minutes**

1. Open **Admin** and review the identity list for anyone holding more access than their job needs.
2. Read the recent audit activity for anything unexpected.
3. Open **Settings** and confirm the areas that should be switched on are on.
4. Check every faculty has at least one coordinator who can approve student work.

**Key screens:** Admin (roles, identity, audit log), Settings (switching whole areas on or off).

**What you can see:** everything, which is exactly why your actions are logged. Give people the narrowest role that lets them do their job.

### 3.11 Employer

**Who you are:** you represent an organisation that hires QIU students and graduates.

**Your first five minutes**

1. Open **Settings** and check your organisation name and contact details are right.
2. Open **Employers** and post your first vacancy.
3. Open **Talent Feed** and filter for the skills you need.
4. Invite a student to interview through the platform, so CPDC can support them through it.

**Key screens:** Employers (posting vacancies, seeing applicants), Talent Feed (student projects published with consent), Workshops (offering to run a session).

**What you can see:** only what students opted in to publish and a QIU coordinator approved. Not grades, not contact details, and not any student who has not opted in.

### 3.12 Workplace Supervisor (no account)

**Who you are:** you supervise a QIU student at your workplace. You have no account here and you do not need one.

**What to do**

1. Wait for the email from QIU that arrives when your student submits their hours.
2. Open the link in that email. It shows one entry: the date, the hours, and what the student says they did.
3. Read it and check it matches what the student actually worked.
4. Press **Approve** if it is correct.
5. Do not press Approve if anything is wrong. Reply to the student instead and ask them to correct and resubmit.
6. Close the page when the confirmation appears.

The link works once and then expires. It gives you no ongoing access to QIU systems and no access to any other student's information.

## 4. How to do each task

### 4.1 Set up your profile (students)

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Check your faculty and programme. A wrong faculty means wrong recommendations everywhere.
3. Add one or two career targets.
4. Add the industries you would consider working in.
5. Add any links you want employers to see, for example a portfolio.
6. Press save.

**What happens next:** your Dashboard, Career Path and workshop suggestions rebuild around your answers straight away.

### 4.2 Work out what to do next (students)

1. Open **Dashboard**.
2. Read the **Your career pathway** section at the top.
3. Select the highlighted next step to open its dedicated module page.
4. Finish it, then return to Dashboard to see your progress update.

Completing tasks also adds XP (points you earn for career activities), which appear on your profile.

### 4.3 Follow your year-by-year plan (students)

1. Open **Career Path**.
2. Find the year you are in now.
3. Read the actions for that year. Items marked required are the ones not to skip.
4. Press any action to open the screen where you do it.
5. Look at the next year so nothing is a surprise.

### 4.4 Find out which skills you are missing (students)

1. Open **Diagnostics**.
2. Pick the diagnostic for your faculty.
3. Answer honestly. Guessing high hides the gaps and wastes your own time.
4. Finish the test and read your results.
5. Press through to the suggested workshops.

**What happens next:** your results become a short list of workshops that close exactly those gaps, and you earn XP. You can retake the test later to show improvement.

### 4.5 Book a workshop

1. Open **Workshops**.
2. Read the list. Sessions matched to your faculty and diagnostic results appear first.
3. Press a session to read what it covers, who runs it, and where it is.
4. Press register.
5. Put the date in your own calendar — the platform records your place, it does not remind your phone.

Seats are limited. If a session is full, the register button is not available.

### 4.6 Check in at a workshop

1. Arrive and find the QR code the organiser displays. (A QR code is the square black-and-white pattern you point a phone camera at.)
2. Open **Workshops** on your phone.
3. Press the check-in button and point your camera at the code.
4. Wait for the confirmation on your screen.
5. Tell the organiser straight away if the scan does not work, while you are still in the room.

**What happens next:** attendance is recorded immediately, you earn XP, and the session appears on your CV. It cannot be added afterwards by scanning from home.

### 4.7 Find online courses worth doing

1. Open **Learning**.
2. Read the learning paths first — a path is an ordered set of courses that build on each other.
3. Pick the path or course that closes the gap you are working on, rather than collecting unrelated certificates.
4. Press through to the provider and do the course there. The course happens on the provider's website, not here.
5. Come back when you have your certificate.

### 4.8 Get a course certificate onto your record

1. Open **Learning**.
2. Find the course and press the option to submit your certificate.
3. Choose how to prove it: paste the link the provider gave you, or upload the file.
4. Check the link opens for someone who is not signed in to your account. A private link cannot be verified.
5. Press submit for verification.

**What happens next:** your submission joins the CPDC verification queue. It becomes verified when accepted, or comes back with a note asking for better evidence.

### 4.9 Add your placement or internship hours (students)

1. Open **Logbook**.
2. Fill in the date, the number of hours, and a short description of what you actually did.
3. Type your workplace supervisor's name.
4. Type your workplace supervisor's email address, and check every character. This is where the approval request is sent.
5. Press submit.

**What happens next:** the entry is saved as waiting for approval and an email goes to your supervisor. The hours do not count towards your record or your CV until they approve.

### 4.10 Chase a supervisor approval (students)

1. Open **Logbook** and check the status on the entry.
2. Ask your supervisor to check their inbox, then their spam or junk folder, for an email from QIU about approving a logbook entry.
3. Check the email address on the entry is exactly right. One wrong letter means the email went nowhere.
4. Tell your supervisor the link works once and expires, so they should open it on a device they can finish on.
5. If the link expired or the address was wrong, contact [CPDC contact — to be filled in by CPDC] to have a new request sent.

### 4.11 Review placement hours (staff)

1. Open **Logbook**.
2. Read the list. You see only students within your own scope.
3. Scan for entries still marked pending and note how long they have waited.
4. Contact the student where a workplace supervisor has not responded, so the entry can be re-sent before the deadline.
5. Use the totals to check a student is on track for the hours their programme requires.

Nothing here replaces the workplace supervisor's approval — that has to come from the person who saw the work.

### 4.12 Publish your Final Year Project (students)

1. Open **FYP**. (FYP stands for Final Year Project.)
2. Choose how to enter it: upload your project poster as an image, or answer the guided questions if you do not have a poster.
3. Write the title and a short description. Name the actual tools and skills you used — employers search on those words.
4. Press submit.
5. Wait for your coordinator to review it.

**What happens next:** it goes to your faculty coordinator first. Nobody outside QIU sees it until it is approved. Once approved it appears on your CV, and in the Talent Feed if you opted in.

### 4.13 Approve a Final Year Project (staff)

1. Open **FYP** or **Admin**, depending on your role, and find the submissions waiting for review.
2. Open a submission and read the title, description and poster.
3. Check it is the student's own work and that nothing confidential from a partner organisation is included.
4. Check the listed skills and tools are accurate, because employers search on those words.
5. Approve it, or return it with a note saying what needs changing.

### 4.14 Verify student certificates (staff)

1. Open **Learning** and go to the certificate verification queue.
2. Open the evidence link or file for the first submission.
3. Check three things: the student's name on the certificate, the course title, and the issuing provider.
4. Accept if all three match.
5. Return it for more evidence if anything is unclear, and write what is missing. The student reads your note, so make it specific.

### 4.15 Get your CV (students)

1. Open **My CV**.
2. Read the draft. Sections are filled in from your verified activity, so the page is not blank.
3. Edit the summary paragraph at the top in your own words.
4. Check every entry is something you actually did. Report anything wrong rather than deleting it, because the record behind it may also need correcting.
5. Press download or export to save it as a PDF.

The PDF has selectable text, which matters because employers run CVs through ATS software (the software employers use to scan CVs before a human reads them), and that software cannot read a picture of text.

### 4.16 Practise for an interview (students)

1. Open **Interview Practice**.
2. Choose the job role you are aiming at.
3. Read the skill comparison — what that role usually expects, next to what you have evidence for.
4. Press to start a timed practice question.
5. Answer out loud, then stop the timer to read the feedback.
6. Repeat with a different question. Practising three times beats reading the advice once.

Practice sessions are for you. They are not shared with employers and they are not a graded assessment.

### 4.17 Find and apply for a job (students and alumni)

1. Open **Employers**.
2. Filter by the type of role you want.
3. Open a role and read what it asks for.
4. Check your CV is up to date, because your CV is what gets sent.
5. Press apply.

### 4.18 Post a vacancy (employers)

1. Open **Employers**.
2. Press the option to post a vacancy.
3. Choose the type of role: internship, graduate or part-time.
4. Write the title, what the person will do, and what you need them to already know.
5. Press post.
6. Return to the same screen to see who has applied.

### 4.19 Find students to hire (employers)

1. Open **Talent Feed**.
2. Filter by faculty or by the skills you need.
3. Open a project to read what the student built and which tools they used.
4. Press the option to invite the student to interview.
5. Write what the next step is and roughly when. A vague invitation gets a vague response.

Invitations go through CPDC rather than straight to the student, so the university can support them through the process.

### 4.20 Find an alumni mentor (students)

1. Open **Network**.
2. Filter by industry, role or faculty.
3. Open a profile and read their background before writing anything.
4. Press to send a connection request, and write a short note saying who you are and what you want to ask. A blank request cannot be sent.
5. If they do not reply within a week, ask someone else rather than sending a second message.

To book time once they accept: open their profile, press to book a slot, choose a time they published, write the one topic you want advice on, and confirm.

### 4.21 Publish a workshop (staff)

1. Open **Workshops** and find the option to publish a workshop.
2. Write a title that says what a student will be able to do afterwards, not the internal project name.
3. Write a short description of what the session covers.
4. Enter the speaker, the date and time, the venue or online link, and the number of seats.
5. Check the date and time twice. Students book against exactly what you enter.
6. Press publish.

On the day, display the check-in QR code so attendance is recorded.

### 4.22 Change what someone is allowed to see (Super-Admin)

1. Open **Admin** and go to the identity section.
2. Find the person by name or email address.
3. Read what their current role allows before changing it.
4. Choose the narrowest role that still lets them do their job.
5. Save, and tell the person — their menu will look different next time they sign in.

The change takes effect at once and is recorded in the audit log with your name against it.

### 4.23 Turn a whole area on or off (Super-Admin)

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Find the section listing the platform areas.
3. Turn an area off to hide it from every menu and block its address.
4. Tell the staff who use it before you turn it off, not afterwards.
5. Turn it back on to restore it. Nothing is deleted while an area is switched off.

### 4.24 Approve a new employer partner (CPDC)

1. Open **Admin** and find the employer records.
2. Check the organisation is real and that the contact works there.
3. Approve if the checks pass.
4. Leave it unapproved and note why if anything does not add up.

An unapproved employer can neither post vacancies nor browse the Talent Feed.

## 5. When something is not working

**I cannot sign in.**
1. Check the email address for a stray space at the end.
2. Check you are using the address the university registered, not a personal one.
3. Try once more, slowly.
4. Contact [CPDC contact — to be filled in by CPDC] if it still fails. Do not create a second account.

**A menu item someone told me about is not there.**
1. Check your role — each role sees a different menu on purpose.
2. Ask the person who told you about it which role they have.
3. Contact [CPDC contact — to be filled in by CPDC] if you believe your role is wrong for your job.

**My workplace supervisor never got the approval email.**
1. Ask them to check their spam or junk folder.
2. Open Logbook and read back the email address on the entry, character by character.
3. Ask [CPDC contact — to be filled in by CPDC] to send a fresh request if the address was wrong or the link expired.

**I attended a workshop but it is not on my record.**
1. Check whether you scanned the check-in code on the day. Booking a place does not record attendance.
2. Tell the person who ran the session — they hold the attendance list.
3. Scan on the day next time, before you leave the room.

**My certificate was returned for more evidence.**
1. Read the note on the returned submission — it says what was missing.
2. Check the certificate shows your full name, the course title and the provider.
3. Check the link opens for someone not signed in to your account.
4. Submit it again with better evidence.

**My CV looks empty.**
1. Remember only verified activity appears. A new account has nothing to show yet.
2. Take the diagnostic, attend one workshop and check in — the CV starts filling itself.
3. Add your own summary paragraph in the meantime so the page is not blank when you export it.

**A page is blank or stuck loading.**
1. Reload the page once.
2. Check your internet connection.
3. Sign out and sign back in.
4. Report it to [CPDC contact — to be filled in by CPDC] with the page name and the time, if it keeps failing.

**Everything is showing the wrong faculty or programme.**
1. Open Settings and check the faculty and programme fields.
2. Correct them and save.
3. Contact [CPDC contact — to be filled in by CPDC] if the correct option is not offered.

**I want employers to stop seeing my profile.**
1. Open Settings and go to the visibility section.
2. Turn off the option that lists you in the Talent Feed, and save.
3. QIU staff responsible for you can still see your record, because they need it to support and assess you.

## 6. What the words mean

| Word | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **ATS** | The software employers use to scan CVs before a person reads them. It cannot read a picture of text, which is why your CV is exported as real, selectable text. |
| **Badge** | A small marker on your profile showing you completed something specific, such as a diagnostic or a verified course. |
| **CPDC** | The QIU Career and Professional Development Centre — the university team that runs this platform, the workshops and the employer partnerships. |
| **Career Path** | A year-by-year list of the career things to do during your degree, tailored to your faculty. |
| **Check-in** | Scanning the code at a workshop to record that you were actually there. Booking a place is not the same as attending. |
| **Diagnostic** | A short skills test whose only job is to point you at the right workshops. |
| **FYP** | Your Final Year Project — the major piece of work you produce in your last year. |
| **Logbook** | The official record of the hours you worked on placement or internship, approved by your workplace supervisor. |
| **Magic link** | A one-time web link sent by email that lets someone do one specific thing without creating an account. It stops working after it is used. |
| **Module** | One area of this platform, such as Logbook or Workshops. It does not mean a taught module on your degree. |
| **QR code** | The square black-and-white pattern you point a phone camera at. Used to check in at workshops. |
| **Role** | What kind of account you have. Your role decides which parts of the platform you can open. |
| **Scope** | How much a staff member is allowed to see — their own mentees, their programme, their faculty, or the whole university. |
| **Talent Feed** | The list of student projects employers can browse. Students appear only if they opted in and a coordinator approved the project. |
| **Verified** | Checked by a person at QIU. Only verified activity reaches your CV, which is what makes the CV worth something. |
| **XP** | Points you earn for career activities such as attending a workshop or completing a diagnostic. They track your effort over time. |

## 7. Privacy: who can see what

There are three layers.

**Only you.** Your draft CV before you export it, your interview practice sessions, your diagnostic answers, and anything you have not submitted.

**You and the QIU staff responsible for you.** Your progress, your placement hours, your submissions and your readiness figures. Which staff depends on their scope: an Academic Supervisor sees assigned students, a Faculty Coordinator sees their faculty, and CPDC roles see the university. Staff access to student records is written to an audit log. You cannot switch this layer off, in the same way you could not opt out of a paper record held by your faculty.

**Employers and outside viewers.** Only what you opted in to publish *and* a coordinator approved. Turning off Talent Feed visibility in Settings removes you from employer searches immediately.

A workplace supervisor approving your hours sees one entry from one student, through a link that expires after a single use. They see nothing else.

For the full written statement, open the privacy page on the platform.
